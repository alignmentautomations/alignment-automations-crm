# Sales Script — Done-for-You Intake System

A flexible call/sales guide for selling the **$1,500 setup + $297/mo** managed package
to home service contractors (painters, HVAC, roofers, plumbers). It's a guide, not a
read-word-for-word script — stay conversational, listen more than you talk, and let
their answers steer it.

**The one big idea to sell:** *You stay on the job. We make sure no lead slips through —
and you never have to touch the tech.*

---

## 0. Before the call (30 seconds of prep)
- [ ] Look at their current website (if any) and how a lead would reach them.
- [ ] Try their contact form or note if there's no easy way to book.
- [ ] Know their trade so your examples are relevant.

---

## 1. Open — set the tone (under a minute)
> "Thanks for making the time. This'll be quick — like 15 minutes. I just want to understand how leads reach you today and where they might be slipping, and then I'll show you exactly what I'd set up. No pressure, no hard sell. Sound good?"

Goal: lower their guard, set the agenda, make it about *them*, not a pitch.

---

## 2. Discover — find the pain (most of the call)
Ask, then shut up and listen. Take notes on their exact words — you'll repeat them back later.

- "Walk me through what happens when a new customer tries to reach you right now."
- "When you're up on a ladder / under a sink and the phone rings — what happens to that call?"
- "Do you ever call someone back and they've already hired somebody else?"
- "How are you keeping track of who reached out and whether you followed up?"
- "Roughly what's a typical job worth to you?" *(anchors the value math later)*
- "If you missed, say, 3 calls a week — what do you think that's costing you?"

**Listen for the money line.** When they say something like *"Yeah, I lose jobs because I can't get to the phone"* — that's the pain you'll sell to. Write it down verbatim.

---

## 3. Reframe — name the real problem
> "Here's the thing most contractors don't realize: you don't have a *lead* problem. You have an *intake* problem. The jobs are coming in — they're just slipping through between the call and the callback. And the job almost always goes to whoever responds first, not whoever's the best at the work. You're losing to slower competitors with faster systems."

This shifts it from "do I need marketing?" to "I'm already losing money I could keep."

---

## 4. Present — paint the picture (keep it about outcomes, not tech)
> "So here's what I set up for you. A customer fills out your site or calls you. If you can't pick up, they instantly get a text back — before they call the next guy. They get a link to book an estimate right on your calendar. You get a notification with their info, and every lead is logged automatically. All of it runs 24/7, even at 9pm on a Sunday."

Then the kicker — the differentiator:
> "And the part that matters most for you: **you don't touch any of it.** No logins, no dashboards, no accounts to manage. I set it all up, I run it, I keep it working. You just keep doing the work and showing up to the estimates that land on your calendar."

*(If helpful, walk them through the live demo flow on how-it-works so they can see it.)*

---

## 5. Price — say it plainly and stop talking
> "It's one simple plan. **$1,500 to set everything up, then $297 a month** — and that covers everything: the website, the text-back, the follow-ups, the booking, the software it all runs on, and me keeping it running. No surprise add-ons. No long-term contract — you can cancel anytime and it's yours."

Then **stop.** Let the silence sit. Don't fill it.

**Value math (only if they flinch on price):**
> "If a typical job is worth $[their number], the system pays for itself the first month it saves you even one or two jobs you'd have otherwise lost. After that it's just profit you were leaving on the table."

---

## 6. Handle the common objections

**"I already have a website."**
> "Perfect — if it can support the system, we build right around it, same price. If it can't, I'll rebuild it at no extra charge. The plan's for the whole intake system, not just the web page."

**"That's more than I expected / it's a lot."**
> "I hear you. Quick question — what's one job worth to you? … Right, so this is less than one job a month, and it's built to win you more than that. It's not a cost, it's the thing that stops the bleeding."

**"Can I just get the text-back part?"**
> "I get why you'd ask. The reason it's one system is the pieces only work together — a text-back with nowhere to book just creates a second missed step. The website, the follow-up, and the booking are what actually turn that text into a job on your calendar. That's what you're paying for."

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Totally fair. Can I ask — is it the price, the timing, or are you just not sure it'll work for your business? … " *(Find the real objection. Then address that one.)*

**"How do I know it'll work?"**
> "The instant response starts working the day we turn it on — your response time goes from hours to seconds immediately. Most folks notice more booked estimates within the first few weeks. And there's no lock-in, so the risk is on me to keep earning it."

**"I'm not tech-y."**
> "That's exactly who this is built for. There's nothing for you to run. If you can read a text and check your calendar, you're set. I handle everything else."

---

## 7. Close — assume the next step
Don't ask "do you want to buy?" Ask a forward-motion question:
> "I think this is a great fit for you. The setup takes about 5–7 days once I have what I need. Do you want to get started, or do you have any questions I haven't covered?"

If yes:
> "Great. I'll send over the agreement and the first invoice, plus a short intake form so I can grab your logo, photos, and a few details. Once that's back, I get to work and you'll be live within the week."

If "let me think":
> "No problem. I'll send you a quick summary so you have everything in writing. Can I check back in on [specific day]?" *(Always set the next contact — never leave it open.)*

---

## Tone reminders
- **Talk like a contractor, not a software company.** Plain words. No "leverage," "solutions," "ecosystem."
- **Sell the outcome** (more booked jobs, zero hassle), not the features.
- **Confidence, not pressure.** No lock-in means you can be relaxed — the system earns its keep.
- **Listen 70%, talk 30%.** The best close is repeating their own pain back to them.
